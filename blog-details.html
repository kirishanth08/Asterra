What Is Notarization and When Do You Need It?

A practical guide to notarization, identity verification, signatures and the situations where a notarized document may be required.

5 min read  ·  Notarization  ·  Asterra Insights

Notarization is a formal process that helps verify a signer's identity and the proper execution of a document. It is commonly used for personal, legal and business documents when an organization or authority requires additional formal verification.

What does notarization mean?

A notary generally verifies the signer's identity, confirms that the person is signing voluntarily, witnesses the signature when required, and completes the applicable notarial certificate or seal. Notarization confirms the formal signing process; it does not automatically confirm that every statement in the document is true.

When do you need a notarized document?

You may need notarization when a receiving authority specifically asks for it or when a document requires formal signature verification.

Affidavits

Declarations or statements that require formal execution.

Power of Attorney

Documents authorizing another person to act on your behalf.

Authorization Letters

Formal permissions used by another person or organization.

Business Documents

Certain agreements and declarations may require notarization.

What should you bring?

  • Original document requiring notarization.
  • Valid government-issued identification.
  • Supporting documents requested by the receiving authority.
  • All required signers, if more than one person must sign.
  • Destination requirements if the document will be used abroad.
Important: If the notary must witness your signature, do not sign the document beforehand.

Notarization, apostille and attestation

These are different processes. Notarization focuses on the formal signing or certification of a document. Apostille and embassy attestation are additional authentication routes that may be required when documents are being used internationally. Always confirm the destination requirements before processing.

Quick preparation checklist

Confirm the required format, prepare your identification, bring all supporting paperwork, and check whether any additional authentication is needed. Good preparation can prevent delays and repeat visits.
